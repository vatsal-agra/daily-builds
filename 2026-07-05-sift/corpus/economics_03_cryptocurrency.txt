How Cryptocurrency Networks Reach Agreement

Cryptocurrencies solve a problem traditional digital cash could not: preventing the same unit of currency from being spent twice without a trusted central authority keeping the ledger. Bitcoin's proof-of-work system has network participants compete to solve a computationally expensive puzzle, and whoever solves it first gets to add the next block of transactions, making it economically impractical to rewrite history without controlling more computing power than everyone else combined. Newer systems use proof-of-stake instead, where the right to add the next block is weighted by how much currency a participant has locked up as collateral, using far less electricity than proof-of-work mining. Regardless of the consensus mechanism, every full participant in the network keeps a copy of the entire transaction history, which is what lets the network detect and reject a fraudulent double-spend attempt without needing a central bank.
