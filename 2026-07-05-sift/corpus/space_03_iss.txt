Life Aboard the International Space Station

The International Space Station has been continuously occupied since November 2000, orbiting Earth roughly every ninety minutes at about 400 kilometers altitude. Crews conduct experiments in microgravity that are impossible on the ground, from protein crystal growth to combustion studies to monitoring how the human body adapts to weeks without gravity, including bone density loss and fluid shifts toward the head. Water aboard the station is recycled extensively, including humidity condensate and even treated urine, because launching water from Earth is expensive. Astronauts exercise roughly two hours daily on a treadmill and resistance machine to offset the muscle and bone loss caused by long-duration spaceflight.
