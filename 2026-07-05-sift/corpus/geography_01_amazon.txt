The Amazon Rainforest's Role in Global Climate

The Amazon basin holds roughly ten percent of the species known on Earth and generates a significant share of its own rainfall through evapotranspiration, water pulled up by tree roots and released into the air, which then falls again over the forest further inland. This cycle means large-scale deforestation does not just remove trees locally, it can reduce rainfall in areas hundreds of kilometers away that depend on moisture recycled from the forest upwind. Parts of the Amazon have already shifted from being a net absorber of atmospheric carbon dioxide to a net source, as fires and clearing release carbon faster than the remaining forest can reabsorb it. The river system itself carries more water than the next several largest rivers in the world combined.
