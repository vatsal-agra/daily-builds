The Science of Baking Bread

Bread rises because yeast ferments sugars in the dough, releasing carbon dioxide that gets trapped in a network of gluten formed when flour proteins are hydrated and stretched by kneading. A long, cold fermentation in the refrigerator develops more flavor than a fast rise at room temperature, because enzymes have more time to break starches into flavorful sugars. Steam in the oven during the first few minutes of baking keeps the crust soft long enough for the loaf to expand fully before it sets, which is why bakers throw ice cubes into a hot oven or bake inside a covered pot. Bread is done when its internal temperature reaches about 96 degrees Celsius, not by crust color alone.
