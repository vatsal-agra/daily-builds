Black Holes and the Event Horizon

A black hole forms when enough mass collapses into a small enough volume that its escape velocity exceeds the speed of light, creating an event horizon beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape. Despite the name, a black hole is not a hole in space but an extremely dense concentration of mass whose gravity warps the surrounding spacetime severely enough that time itself passes more slowly near the horizon, an effect confirmed by observing light from matter spiraling in. In 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope produced the first direct image of a black hole's shadow, in the galaxy M87, by combining data from radio telescopes spread across the planet into a single virtual instrument the size of Earth. Stephen Hawking showed that black holes should slowly radiate energy through a quantum effect now called Hawking radiation, implying that, over an extraordinarily long timescale, they eventually evaporate.
