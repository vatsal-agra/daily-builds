Roman Aqueducts and Ancient Engineering

Roman aqueducts moved water across tens of kilometers using nothing but a carefully maintained downward gradient, often as gentle as a few centimeters of drop per kilometer, calculated without modern surveying instruments. Most of an aqueduct's length ran underground or at ground level, with the famous elevated arched sections, like the Pont du Gard, built only where the terrain required crossing a valley. Lead and terracotta pipes distributed water within cities to public fountains, baths, and the homes of wealthier residents. Maintenance crews called aquarii cleared mineral buildup and repaired leaks, and the office of Rome's water commissioner, held for a time by Frontinus, left behind a detailed written record of the whole network.
