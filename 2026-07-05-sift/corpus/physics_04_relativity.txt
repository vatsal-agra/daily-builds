Special and General Relativity, Briefly

Special relativity starts from a simple observation: the speed of light in a vacuum is measured as the same value by every observer regardless of how fast they are moving, and Einstein showed that accepting this forces time and space measurements to depend on an observer's velocity, producing effects like time dilation, where a fast-moving clock ticks slower as measured from a stationary frame. General relativity extends this by describing gravity not as a force but as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy, so a planet orbits a star because it is following the straightest possible path through curved spacetime rather than being pulled by an invisible force. GPS satellites must correct for both effects daily: their speed causes their clocks to run slightly slow, while the weaker gravity at orbital altitude causes their clocks to run slightly fast, and the two corrections do not cancel out evenly.
