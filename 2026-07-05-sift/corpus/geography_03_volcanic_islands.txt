How Volcanic Islands Form and Disappear

Chains of volcanic islands like Hawaii form as a tectonic plate slides over a stationary hotspot of rising magma deep in the mantle, so the youngest island sits directly above the hotspot while older islands further along the chain are progressively more eroded and eventually sink below the waves entirely. A newly formed volcanic island starts as bare rock, and its first colonizers are typically wind-blown spores and seeds, followed by birds that deposit seeds from other locations in their droppings. Coral often begins growing in the shallow water surrounding a volcanic island, and if the island subsides slowly enough, the coral can keep growing upward, eventually leaving a ring-shaped atoll after the original volcanic peak has disappeared beneath the surface.
