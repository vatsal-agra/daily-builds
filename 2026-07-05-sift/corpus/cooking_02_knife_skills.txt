Knife Skills Every Home Cook Should Learn

A sharp knife is safer than a dull one because it requires less pressure and is less likely to slip. The claw grip, curling fingertips under and guiding the blade against the first knuckle, protects fingers while allowing fast, even cuts. A rocking motion with a chef's knife, keeping the tip on the board, makes mincing herbs and garlic faster than repeated straight chops. Dicing an onion evenly starts with a flat cut base so it sits stable, followed by horizontal and vertical cuts before slicing across. Honing a knife on a steel before each use realigns the edge, while actual sharpening on a stone removes metal and should happen only every few weeks depending on use.
