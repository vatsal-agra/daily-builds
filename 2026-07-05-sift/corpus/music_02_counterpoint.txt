Counterpoint and the Art of Independent Melodic Lines

Counterpoint is the technique of combining two or more melodic lines that are harmonically interdependent yet rhythmically and melodically independent, most rigorously codified in the rules Johann Joseph Fux laid out for species counterpoint. In first species, one note is written against each note of a given melody, restricted mostly to consonant intervals, building toward more elaborate species that permit passing tones and syncopation. Bach's fugues take counterpoint to its most developed form, weaving a single subject through multiple voices in imitation, sometimes inverted or played at different speeds simultaneously. Studying species counterpoint remains a standard part of music theory education because its constraints force careful attention to how independent lines interact.
