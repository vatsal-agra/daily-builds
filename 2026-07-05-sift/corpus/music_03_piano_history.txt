The Invention and Rise of the Piano

The piano was invented around 1700 by Bartolomeo Cristofori, who wanted an instrument that could play both loud and soft, hence its original name pianoforte, unlike the harpsichord whose plucked strings cannot vary in volume based on touch. Cristofori's hammer mechanism let a player control dynamics directly through how hard a key was struck, and later refinements added a sustain pedal that lifts dampers off the strings. The piano's dynamic range made it central to Romantic-era composers like Chopin and Liszt, who wrote music that relies on dramatic swells from whisper-quiet to thunderous. Iron frames, introduced in the nineteenth century, let pianos hold far higher string tension than earlier wooden frames could survive, producing a louder, more sustained tone.
