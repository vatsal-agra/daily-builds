Reading Climate History in Glacier Ice

Glaciers form where snowfall each year exceeds summer melt, compacting into dense ice that preserves tiny air bubbles trapped at the moment each layer formed, effectively sealing a sample of the ancient atmosphere. Scientists drill ice cores and count annual layers like tree rings, then measure the trapped gas and the ratio of oxygen isotopes in the ice itself to reconstruct temperature going back hundreds of thousands of years. Glaciers also flow slowly downhill under their own weight, carving valleys and depositing rocky debris called moraines that mark how far the ice once extended. Most of the world's mountain glaciers are currently retreating, and because many communities depend on glacial meltwater for drinking water and irrigation during dry seasons, their disappearance threatens water supplies well beyond the mountains themselves.
