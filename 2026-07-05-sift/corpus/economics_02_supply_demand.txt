Supply and Demand Beyond the Textbook Diagram

The textbook supply and demand curve describes an idealized market where price alone clears the quantity buyers want against the quantity sellers offer, but real markets are shaped heavily by how elastic each curve is: necessities like insulin have inelastic demand, so price changes only weakly affect how much people buy. Price controls, like rent caps, can hold prices below the level where supply and demand would naturally meet, which economic theory predicts creates a persistent shortage rather than solving the underlying scarcity. Externalities complicate the picture further: a factory's pollution imposes costs on people who are not part of the transaction at all, which is why economists argue unregulated markets underprice goods whose production harms bystanders. Supply and demand still describes the underlying pressure in almost every market, even when regulation or externalities prevent prices from settling exactly where the simple model predicts.
