Regional Italian Pasta Traditions

Fresh egg pasta, common in Italy's north, is rolled thin from flour and egg dough and pairs well with butter and cream sauces, while dried durum wheat pasta from the south is extruded and holds up to long cooking in tomato-based sauces. Bologna's ragu is simmered for hours with soffritto, wine, and milk, and is traditionally served with tagliatelle rather than spaghetti. Rome contributes four pasta dishes built from the same short list of ingredients: cacio e pepe with just cheese and pepper, carbonara adding egg and cured pork, gricia, and amatriciana with tomato. Pasta water, starchy from the cooking pasta, is often added to a sauce off the heat to help it emulsify and cling to the noodles.
