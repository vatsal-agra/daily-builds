The Sahara Was Not Always a Desert

Rock art found deep in today's Sahara desert depicts swimmers, hippos, and cattle herding, evidence that the region was a green savanna with lakes as recently as six thousand years ago, a period called the African Humid Period. Slow, cyclical changes in Earth's orbital tilt shifted the West African monsoon's reach, and as rainfall declined the vegetation that had been helping sustain local rainfall disappeared too, accelerating the shift toward desert. Today the Sahara's dust, picked up by strong winds, travels across the Atlantic in visible plumes and deposits nutrients like phosphorus in the Amazon basin, an ocean away. Despite its extreme conditions, the Sahara supports specially adapted life, from addax antelope that can survive without ever drinking free water to nomadic human communities with millennia of accumulated desert knowledge.
