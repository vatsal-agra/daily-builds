How Jazz Improvisation Actually Works

Jazz improvisation is rarely pure invention; most soloists build phrases from a vocabulary of licks and patterns practiced over the chord changes of a tune until they can be recombined instantly in real time. Players think in terms of the underlying chord-scale relationships, choosing notes that fit each chord as it passes rather than treating the whole tune as one static key. Call and response between soloists, or between a soloist and the rhythm section, creates the conversational feel associated with the genre. Bebop musicians in the 1940s pushed harmonic complexity further by adding extra chord changes between the original chords of a tune, forcing faster and more precise navigation of the harmony than earlier swing era improvisation required.
