Chess as Competitive Sport

Top-level chess is played under strict time controls, often ninety minutes plus a small increment per move, and players can lose on time even in a completely winning position. Opening preparation now extends twenty or more moves deep, memorized from computer analysis, which has shifted competitive advantage toward whoever has studied a specific line most recently. The Elo rating system, adapted from statistics used to rank other competitors, estimates a player's strength from match results and predicts the expected score of future games. Chess engines now vastly exceed human playing strength, and top players use them extensively for preparation and post-game analysis, even though using one during a game is banned and grounds for disqualification.
