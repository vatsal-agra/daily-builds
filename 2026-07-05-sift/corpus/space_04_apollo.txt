The Apollo Program and the First Moon Landings

The Apollo program put twelve astronauts on the Moon between 1969 and 1972, using the Saturn V, still the most powerful rocket ever successfully flown. Apollo 11's lunar module Eagle landed with only seconds of fuel remaining after computer alarms forced Neil Armstrong to manually pilot past a boulder field. Apollo 13's mission was aborted after an oxygen tank explosion, and the crew survived by using the lunar module as a lifeboat, a rescue improvised in real time by engineers on the ground. Later missions carried a battery-powered lunar rover, extending how far astronauts could travel to collect rock samples that still inform theories of how the Moon formed.
