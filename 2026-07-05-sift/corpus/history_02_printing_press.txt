The Printing Press and the Spread of Ideas

Johannes Gutenberg's mid-fifteenth-century printing press combined movable metal type, oil-based ink, and a modified wine press into a system that could reproduce a page far faster than hand copying. Within decades, print shops had spread across Europe, and the price of books fell enough that ownership extended well beyond monasteries and royal courts. The press accelerated the Reformation, letting Martin Luther's arguments circulate widely within weeks rather than years, and standardized spelling and grammar as regional printers settled on consistent conventions. It also enabled the rapid spread of scientific work, since researchers could cite a shared, identical printed text rather than a hand-copied manuscript that might contain transcription errors.
