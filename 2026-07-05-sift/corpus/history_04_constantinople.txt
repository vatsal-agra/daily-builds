The Fall of Constantinople in 1453

Constantinople's land walls had withstood sieges for over a thousand years before Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II brought enormous bronze cannons, cast specifically for the siege, capable of battering sections of wall that had never before faced artillery of that scale. The defenders, vastly outnumbered, held for weeks, and the Ottomans famously hauled ships overland on greased logs to bypass a chain blocking the Golden Horn harbor. The city's fall ended the Byzantine Empire, the last continuous political link to ancient Rome, and sent many Greek scholars fleeing west with classical manuscripts that helped fuel the Renaissance. Constantinople was renamed Istanbul and became the Ottoman capital for the following centuries.
