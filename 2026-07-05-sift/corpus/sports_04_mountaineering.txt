Altitude and the Challenge of High-Mountain Climbing

Above roughly 5,500 meters, sometimes called the death zone on peaks like Everest, the body cannot acclimatize fully and slowly deteriorates no matter how long a climber stays. Climbers ascend in stages, going high during the day and sleeping lower, a pattern that trains the body to produce more red blood cells before pushing to the next camp. Supplemental oxygen reduces the effective altitude a climber's body experiences, which is why summit success rates and safety statistics differ so much between climbers who use it and those attempting a summit without it. Weather windows on the highest peaks can be narrow, and many expeditions have been forced to turn back within sight of the summit rather than risk descending in a storm.
