Coral Reefs and Their Fragile Symbiosis

Coral reefs are built by tiny animals called polyps that secrete calcium carbonate skeletons, but their color and much of their energy come from microscopic algae called zooxanthellae living inside their tissue in a mutually beneficial relationship. When water temperatures rise even slightly above normal for an extended period, corals expel these algae in a stress response called bleaching, leaving a white skeleton behind; corals can recover if temperatures drop quickly enough, but prolonged bleaching kills them. Reefs cover a tiny fraction of the ocean floor yet support roughly a quarter of all marine species, making them one of the most biodiverse ecosystems on Earth. Ocean acidification, caused by seawater absorbing excess atmospheric carbon dioxide, makes it harder for corals to build their skeletons in the first place.
