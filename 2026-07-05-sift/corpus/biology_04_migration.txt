How Migratory Birds Navigate Thousands of Kilometers

Migratory birds combine several separate navigation systems rather than relying on just one: many can sense Earth's magnetic field directly, likely through a light-sensitive protein in the eye that responds differently depending on magnetic orientation. Birds also use the position of the sun and stars, and some species have been shown to memorize the night sky's rotation around a fixed point rather than any single star. Young birds on their first migration often travel the correct route with no experienced adult to follow, suggesting the general direction and distance are at least partly innate rather than fully learned. Many songbirds migrate at night, when the air is cooler and predators are scarcer, navigating by starlight and calling to each other with short contact calls to stay loosely grouped.
