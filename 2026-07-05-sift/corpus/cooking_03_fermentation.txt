Fermentation Across World Cuisines

Fermentation preserves food and creates flavors that raw ingredients cannot, using microorganisms like lactic acid bacteria, yeast, and molds. Korean kimchi and German sauerkraut both rely on lactic acid bacteria converting cabbage sugars into acid, which lowers the pH enough to prevent spoilage organisms from growing. Japanese miso and soy sauce use koji mold to break down soybean proteins into savory amino acids over months of aging. Sourdough starters are a symbiotic culture of wild yeast and bacteria maintained by regular feeding with flour and water. Even coffee and cacao beans are fermented briefly after harvest, a step that develops much of their final flavor before roasting ever begins.
