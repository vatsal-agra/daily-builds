Photosynthesis at the Molecular Level

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy in two linked stages: light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membrane split water molecules, releasing oxygen and generating energy carrier molecules, while the Calvin cycle in the stroma uses that energy to fix carbon dioxide into sugar. Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light while reflecting green, which is why most leaves appear green to the human eye. Some plants, called C4 plants, evolved a modified pathway that concentrates carbon dioxide before the Calvin cycle, reducing a wasteful process called photorespiration and making them more efficient in hot, dry climates. Nearly all oxygen in Earth's atmosphere ultimately originated from billions of years of photosynthesis by plants and cyanobacteria.
