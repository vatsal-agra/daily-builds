What Stock Prices Are Actually Pricing

A share of stock represents a claim on a company's future profits, so in principle its price should reflect the present value of all the cash the company is expected to pay shareholders from now until it eventually stops existing. In practice, prices move constantly on much shorter-term information, from quarterly earnings reports to interest rate announcements, because investors are constantly revising their estimate of that distant future based on new evidence. Index funds, which simply hold every stock in a market in proportion to its size rather than trying to pick winners, exist because decades of data show most professional stock pickers fail to beat the market average after fees. Volatility, how much a stock's price swings, is not the same thing as risk of permanent loss, though the two are frequently and incorrectly treated as identical in casual conversation.
