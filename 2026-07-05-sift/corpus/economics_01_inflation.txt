What Actually Causes Inflation

Inflation, a sustained rise in the general price level, can come from several directions at once: demand-pull inflation happens when total spending in an economy outpaces its ability to produce goods and services, while cost-push inflation happens when input costs like wages or energy rise and get passed on to consumers. Central banks primarily fight inflation by raising interest rates, which makes borrowing more expensive and cools spending on big purchases like homes and cars, gradually reducing demand-side pressure. Inflation expectations matter almost as much as current prices, because if workers and businesses expect high inflation to continue, they build that expectation into wage demands and pricing decisions, which can make inflation self-reinforcing. Not all price increases are inflation in this sense; a single sector's price spike from a supply shock is different from a broad, sustained rise across the economy.
