A from-scratch, packet-level network simulator. Every chart below is real simulator output — cumulative-ACK TCP running over a real discrete-event bottleneck queue — not illustrative data.

1 — Slow start & congestion avoidance

A single bulk flow on a 2 Mbps link, one run per algorithm. Reno and CUBIC grow until a packet loss forces them back (the sawtooth); BBR estimates the link's bandwidth-delay product directly and needs no loss to find it.

2 — AIMD fairness convergence

Two Reno flows, identical RTT, sharing one bottleneck, started 50ms apart. Chiu & Jain's 1989 result: no matter which flow gets a head start, AIMD converges the split toward fair. The right chart is Jain's fairness index measured over rolling 0.5s windows.

3 — RTT unfairness: Reno vs CUBIC

Same topology, two flows with a 4:1 RTT ratio sharing one bottleneck. Reno's per-ACK-clocked growth favors the short-RTT flow; CUBIC's real-time-clocked growth (RFC 8312) narrows that gap. Ratio pooled over 15 independent trials to smooth out single-run RTO-timing noise (see REVIEW.md).

4 — Bufferbloat, and RED's fix for it

A bulk Reno flow shares a deep drop-tail queue with a latency-sensitive "ping" flow (fixed 1-packet window). Drop-tail lets the bulk flow fill the whole buffer before backing off, so the ping flow's RTT balloons. RED drops early and probabilistically, keeping the queue — and the ping flow's RTT — shallow.

5 — BBR vs CUBIC: queue depth at similar throughput

Same generously-buffered bottleneck, both algorithms reach similar link utilization — but CUBIC gets there by keeping the queue nearly full, while BBR paces to the bandwidth-delay product and leaves the queue almost empty. This is BBR's entire reason for existing.
